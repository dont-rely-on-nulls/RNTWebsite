A probabilistic subsystem. Omikuji lets a tuple be likely instead of true:
each uncertain tuple carries a chance, and a question returns its answers
together with how likely each one is, across every world the uncertain
tuples could make.

#+begin_src omikuji
-- each link is up as often as it has been
weigh LINK(src: a, dst: b) by UPTIME(src: a, dst: b, ratio: r) as r.

-- how likely is the edge reachable from the core?
ask chance REACH(origin: 'core-1', dest: 'edge-9').

-- and if the link to core-2 is down?
ask chance REACH(origin: 'core-1', dest: 'edge-9')
  given not LINK(src: 'core-1', dst: 'core-2').
#+end_src

* Why omikuji
At a Japanese shrine you shake a wooden box until one numbered stick falls
out, and the number gives you a paper fortune, from great blessing to
great curse. A fortune is a chance you can draw. Omikuji keeps the box for
every uncertain tuple, and lets you ask what the draws add up to.

* Weights
=weigh= gives a relation’s tuples a chance, either a constant or one computed
from data, like the uptime above. Weighed tuples are independent of each
other. When exactly one of several tuples must hold, =either= says so:

#+begin_src omikuji
weigh LINK(src: 'core-1', dst: 'core-2') as 0.98.

either STATUS(parcel: x, is: 'late') 0.2
    or STATUS(parcel: x, is: 'lost') 0.01
    otherwise STATUS(parcel: x, is: 'on time')
  for each PARCEL(id: x).
#+end_src

Weights are kept beside the data, in =FORTUNE (RELATION, TUPLE, CHANCE)=,
never inside it: =LINK= still holds the same tuples, and every other
subsystem goes on reading it as it always did.

* Possible worlds
Each uncertain tuple is either in or out. One choice for every tuple is a
world, and the chance of a world comes from the weights. A question is
answered in every world at once, and an answer’s chance is the sum over the
worlds where it is an answer.

| Ask                  | Returns                                             |
|----------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| =ask chance φ=         | the probability that φ holds                        |
| =ask φ=                | the answers, each with a =CHANCE= attribute           |
| =ask … given ψ=        | the same, in the worlds where ψ holds               |
| =ask likeliest φ=      | the single most probable world in which φ holds     |
| =draw n φ=             | =n= worlds drawn at random, with each answer’s count  |

* Exact, or drawn
Omikuji answers exactly when it can. It keeps the lineage of every answer,
the uncertain tuples it depends on, and compiles that into a decision
diagram whose probability is quick to read off. The number of worlds is
finite, so every question ends, but it can be very large; for those,
=draw= samples worlds and reports each chance with its margin of error.

#+begin_src omikuji
draw 10000 REACH(origin: 'core-1', dest: e).
#+end_src

* Rules come from elsewhere
Omikuji has no rule language of its own, and needs none. =REACH= above is a
Dandelion bundle:

#+begin_src dandelion
Network:
  Router  key   NAME.
  LINK    from  SRC.
  LINK    to    DST.

REACH:
  ?ORIGIN  LINK+  ?DEST.
#+end_src

Omikuji follows the lineage through the closure, so the chance that one
link is down becomes the chance that a whole region is cut off. The same
works for pureSQL views, Sakura relations and Karuta predicates: anything
that reads uncertain tuples has uncertain answers, and Omikuji knows how
uncertain.

* In the same runtime
An =ask= can be named, and its answers are published under =\relation= with
their =CHANCE=:

#+begin_src omikuji
ODDS: ask REACH(origin: 'core-1', dest: e).
#+end_src

#+begin_src sql
-- from pureSQL: the edges we are least sure we can reach
SELECT DEST, CHANCE FROM ODDS WHERE CHANCE < 0.99 ORDER BY CHANCE;
#+end_src

Aleph’s eggs can be weighed too, and then =ask chance= on =EGG= says how likely
a promise is to hatch before its deadline.
