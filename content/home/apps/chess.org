The board is a relation, =PIECE (SQUARE, KIND, SIDE)=. Karuta generates the
legal moves, the way Prolog has always played chess, and Aleph remembers
what a position cannot: castling rights, en passant and the fifty-move rule.
Positions are content-addressed, so a threefold repetition is the same hash
turning up for the third time.

#+begin_src prolog
knight[[F, R], [F2, R2]] :-
    jump[DF, DR],
    karuta:plus[F, DF, F2], karuta:plus[R, DR, R2],
    board[F2, R2].

jump[1, 2]. jump[2, 1]. jump[-1, 2]. jump[-2, 1].
jump[1, -2]. jump[2, -1]. jump[-1, -2]. jump[-2, -1].
#+end_src
